# Deux Rives Web — Policy for publishing verified client case studies

## Purpose
A well-designed concept is not a client delivery. Do not present studio demos, fictional interfaces, pilot discussions or founder experience as a project commissioned by Deux Rives Web unless the relationship is accurately documented and publication is authorized.

## Required record for each case study
- Project identifier, client name (or accurately described confidential client if approved), sector, location and date range.
- Written publication permission specifying names, logos, imagery, screenshots, quotations and any performance figures that can be disclosed.
- The problem as described at project start; the previous process and operational constraints.
- The exact role and contribution of Deux Rives Web versus the client, contractors or other service providers.
- In-scope architecture, interfaces, integrations, services delivered and implementation dates.
- Evidence for any outcome: measurement definition, baseline, observation period, data source, responsibility for calculation and client confirmation.
- Honest limitations and remaining work, avoiding selectively favorable anecdotes that imply unverified causal impact.
- Accessibility, privacy and legal review for screenshots, photographs and any individual names.
- A designated owner for future corrections or withdrawal requests.

## Suggested public article structure
1. **Client context** — Who needed the system and why. Avoid disclosing confidential processes.
2. **Problem and constraints** — The actual work that was difficult before the engagement.
3. **Our contribution** — Design, engineering, interfaces, integration, deployment and support tasks verifiably completed by the company.
4. **Process and decisions** — Why a particular approach was selected and what alternatives were considered.
5. **Delivered system** — Screenshots or demonstrations with client consent and protection of personal data.
6. **Measured outcomes** — Actual numbers only when independently traceable and approved. Otherwise describe observable results qualitatively without fabricating a percentage.
7. **Next steps and limitations** — Follow-up work, constraints and maintenance status.
8. **Client approval** — Signed-off copy and permitted media.

## Evidence levels
- **Exploration:** Internal concept, fictional records, no customer outcomes.
- **Interactive demonstration:** Functioning browser workflow using synthetic data, no external claims.
- **Pilot:** Limited real-world trial with agreed objectives. Do not claim broad deployment.
- **Delivered client engagement:** Completed work supported by contract/scope, verification and permission to publish.
- **Verified case study:** Client-approved narrative with clear, supported outcome evidence.

## Publication gate
If evidence or consent is missing, keep the page in draft and leave the public References section transparent. Do not invent customers or transform unrelated freelance experience into Deux Rives Web client work.

## Suggested first outreach to real clients
After a real delivery and permission to contact: request consent to describe the original operational challenge, the contribution made and the observations that can be substantiated. Do not pressure a client for favorable reviews. If they decline, respect their decision.
